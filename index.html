Activation-Aware AI Control for Untrusted Coding Agents

A small AI-control testbed for asking whether activation monitoring can catch suspicious coding-agent behavior that code-only monitors miss, and whether combining the two improves safety at a fixed audit budget.

  • Built a 50-task benchmark with clean, toy-backdoored and hard-negative solutions, using public and hidden tests to verify labels by execution.
  • Compared static and LLM-based behavioral monitors with probes trained on hidden-state activations from an open coding model.
  • Implemented combined monitoring and audit protocols, including held-out backdoor families to test robustness under distribution shift.
  • Designed the pipeline to make safety–usefulness trade-offs and monitor failure modes easy to inspect rather than hiding them behind a single aggregate score.

Causal AI Safety Interventions with Sparse Autoencoders

An interpretable-feature pipeline for moving beyond correlation: identify candidate safety-relevant SAE features, intervene on them at inference time, and measure what actually changes.

  • Extracts and mines sparse features from internal activations of small open LLMs, then labels and clusters candidate risk-related features.
  • Supports feature suppression and steering, followed by a sweep over intervention strength.
  • Evaluates safety–utility trade-offs and produces reproducible feature cards for qualitative inspection.
